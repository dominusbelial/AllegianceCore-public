## Native behavior and implementation

M4 lesson 7 permits either any miner's destruction or the current target leaving Mars (`training/mission4.cpp:819-824`). The native advice is to approach cloaked and fire only in range (`:777-794`); the actual prompt uses **F**, despite the older spoken-script comment mentioning E (`:790-800`). The production graph already preserves these rules in `src/Allegiance.ClientCore/Training/Mission4.cs:561-628`. The revised `hunt` pilot cycles real F targeting, keeps the cloak until sniper range, aims through ordinary mouse flight, holds an incoming miner at sniper range with ordinary reverse thrust, and pursues when the damaged miner turns away. It uses the current rendered contact and local flight pose for the existing lead calculation. It fires the authored sniper loadout; it does not add equipment or alter damage.

M8's native Tkela target change requires the trainee's first hull damage (`training/mission8.cpp:603-613`); the omitted constructor fraction defaults to **1.0**, not zero (`training/GetShipIsDamagedCondition.h:32-34`). `src/Allegiance.ClientCore/Training/Mission8.cs:498` incorrectly used zero, immediately interpreting a healthy living trainee as the switch condition. Both accepted and current target conditions now use 1.0, retaining native delayed, once-only execution.

Native wingmen with an accepted station target do not retreat (`Igc/shipIGC.cpp:1730-1747`). The port's `src/Allegiance.Core/Sim/Ship.cs:8839` checked only the older station-data target field, overlooking the model-typed station attack used by the bomber. The exemption now also recognizes the wrapped station target. A damaged wingman attacking a ship still retreats normally. The two encounter corrections have regression tests in `tests/Allegiance.ClientCore.Tests/P917TrainingBranchTests.cs`; each was observed failing against its old condition.

The embedded server also applied multiplayer last-restart-station elimination during training. A live trace showed both remaining defenders killed with no attacker on the exact tick the outpost fell, removing the combat the native ending still observes. Native offline station death delegates to Training and terminates the station, without FedSrv side elimination (`WinTrek/trekigc.cpp:4325-4362`); M8 later supplies its own idle orders (`training/mission8.cpp:629-638`). `src/Allegiance.Server/Server.Lobby.cs:274` now bypasses that multiplayer elimination hook for training missions. The explicit station-loss callback regression fails against the old server behavior; the normal multiplayer station-elimination control remains covered separately.

The shared selected-weapon resolver stopped at the first part sharing the selected mount number. Mount zero can hold a shield, a dispenser and a gun, each in its own equipment class. After swapping in the Nanite gun, a preceding non-weapon part hid it from the lead and range calculation. `src/Allegiance.ClientCore.Game/Rendering/SelectedMountWeapon.cs:29` now finds the weapon by equipment type as native does (`clintlib/clintlib.cpp:1585-1588`). The ordering regression in `tests/Allegiance.ClientCore.MonoGame.Tests/Hud18TargetPaneTests.cs` fails against the old resolver. The correction is shared by Classic and Modern.

Station Goto orders also left the previous repair objective active: a live trace showed accepted Goto on home station 1052 while `RepairTarget` still named bomber 8007. Native accepted autopilot orders replace the single plan (`Igc/shipIGC.h:750-774`). `src/Allegiance.SinglePlayer/Training/TrainingHost.cs:848` now clears the prior objective before installing the station bay plan. `P917TrainingBranchTests.Station_goto_replaces_a_nanite_repair_plan` requires repair to clear while the accepted station and docking plan remain.

The revised `sacrifice` pilot selects the Nanite through the normal commandee-cycle key and sends it to the known home station through a bound default-order action. It leaves the fighters at home, stays within the slow repair projectile's actual moving-target range, uses ordinary thrust keys to match an escort position above the bomber and avoid crossfire, and controls an approach reserve followed by a small firing reserve while the bomber launches its volley. Repair release is timed from the outpost shield and bomber hull; every hit and death remains a real simulation outcome. The bomber retains its authored accepted Attack on the outpost. The `early` pilot mounts through normal equipment input, splits one friendly fighter onto the second miner attacker, conserves energy while targets are healthy, uses current-view mouse aiming through the moving hull centre, and sends all three friendly defenders to escort the bomber after its required first hull hit. Both strict escort policies clear persistent cruise through the normal stop-throttle key so releasing forward thrust actually stops an approach. These are proof-only policies in `src/Allegiance.ClientCore.Game/Game1.TrainingProof.Advanced.cs`; shipping mission conditions and combat recipes remain native.

The observer in `tools/p912-training-proof/Program.cs` reads actual combat outcomes. For M4 it records each miner's last live sector and requires death with zero hull in Mars, without a live target-departure ending. For M8 it requires both actual deaths, the surviving enemy defenders' native DoNothing orders, and the exact final announcement. It observes the entire native five-second final goal (`training/mission8.cpp:646-650`), rather than demanding the bomber already be dead on its first frame. Strict policies select the shipping `--fx native` presentation tier to keep software-rendered input responsive. This retains the real artwork and both HUDs and does not change simulation timing. Real keys, cursor input, selections, equipment swaps and orders still pass through the shipping Game1 handlers. ## Native intent

**Design:** M4 deliberately allows a miner to escape while still advancing the lesson. M8 deliberately permits outpost victory, bomber loss, and the combined sacrifice; the native announcement conditions can observe a late projectile during their timed windows. The port preserves those outcomes.

**Era constraints:** hardcoded actor IDs, narrative delays and overloaded fraction comparisons make intent difficult to distinguish from alive/dead checks. The input pilot must follow the actual current scene while rendering and the server progress independently.

**Bugs:** the five corrected discrepancies were port bugs: an omitted native default became zero, split target representations hid accepted station attacks from the retreat exemption, the weapon resolver confused equipment classes sharing a mount number, multiplayer station-loss elimination leaked into the offline training encounter, and station Goto left a stale repair objective active. They are not changes to native balance or lesson design.

**Modern approach:** explicit Alive/Damaged/Destroyed conditions and one typed accepted-target representation and atomic plan replacement would remove these ambiguities. Event-based outcome observation with a bounded announcement window would make branch proofs easier to diagnose. Such a redesign is outside this parity change; the native timings, combat and messages are retained.
